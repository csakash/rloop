# Scope brief: sleep and procedural memory in older adults

I read this topic as being about how sleep helps consolidate procedural (non-declarative) memory in older adults, and how ageing changes that. It covers both healthy ageing and older adults with age-related conditions such as MCI, Parkinson's disease and obstructive sleep apnea. Key concepts and synonyms: **sleep** (overnight sleep, daytime nap, NREM/SWS, slow oscillations, sleep spindles, spindle–slow-wave coupling, REM, sleep deprivation, sleep quality/fragmentation, sleep apnea); **procedural memory** (procedural learning, motor sequence learning, motor skill learning, motor memory consolidation, motor adaptation, implicit learning, offline gains; typical tasks: finger-tapping / motor sequence task, serial reaction time task, mirror tracing, Tower of Hanoi / cognitive procedural skills); **older adults** (elderly, ageing/aging, healthy older adults, usually aged 60 and over, often compared with young adults). In scope: human behavioural, polysomnography/EEG and neuroimaging studies of sleep-dependent procedural consolidation in older people. Also in scope: age-group comparisons, and interventions that change sleep to change procedural memory, such as targeted memory reactivation, closed-loop acoustic stimulation, transcranial slow-oscillation stimulation, CBT-I, CPAP/oxygen, napping protocols and pharmacological sleep enhancement. Clinical older populations count when a procedural/motor memory outcome is measured, including motor rehabilitation relevance. Out of scope: studies of only declarative or emotional memory, studies of only young adults or children, animal-only mechanism work (as background at most), general sleep–cognition epidemiology without a procedural outcome, and motor learning studies with no sleep variable. Evidence that matters: within-subject or randomised sleep-vs-wake and nap-vs-wake designs, age-comparison experiments with PSG/EEG microstructure correlates, randomised or controlled sleep-modulation intervention trials, and systematic reviews/meta-analyses. Large observational cohorts are secondary unless they include procedural tasks.
