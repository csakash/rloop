### 1. In healthy adults aged 65+, does overnight sleep (vs a circadian-matched wake interval, counterbalanced morning/evening training) produce larger offline gains for a whole-hand or gross-motor sequence task than for an individuated finger-tapping task, and does fine-motor dexterity moderate that difference within subjects?

`g01` · signal: contradiction · score 256 (novelty 4, importance 4, tractability 4, evidence gap 4)

Sleep-vs-wake findings for motor consolidation in older adults point in all directions: no effect (c0026, c0043, c0091), harm (c0044), and benefit when the task uses whole-hand movements instead of individuated fingers (c0092, c0093). Meta-analyses suggest that confounds such as time of testing and reactive inhibition explain much of the reported sleep benefit, and that elderly status is a moderator (c0004, c0006, c0007). No study has crossed task type with a circadian-controlled sleep/wake design in the same older participants, so it is unknown whether the 'age deficit' is really a task-dexterity artefact.
